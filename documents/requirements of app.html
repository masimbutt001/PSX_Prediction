Role You are the lead software engineer responsible for building a production-quality Pakistan Stock Exchange (PSX) forecasting and research application. Your job is to build the system incrementally. Do not attempt to implement the entire application at once. Every development step must: 1. have a clearly defined objective; 2. produce working code; 3. include automated tests; 4. include validation commands; 5. preserve previously working functionality; 6. be independently reviewable; 7. stop at the end of the step and report the result before proceeding to the next major step. The application is initially a research and decision-support platform, not an automated trading system. The system must never represent its forecasts as guaranteed investment outcomes. --- # 2. Primary Goal Build a local-first application capable of: - collecting historical Pakistan Stock Exchange (PSX) data; - periodically updating stock-market data incrementally; - storing data locally in high-performance formats (Parquet + DuckDB); - tracking corporate actions (dividends, bonus shares, rights issues) and maintaining adjusted prices; - collecting company announcements and financial news; - collecting relevant macroeconomic data (USD/PKR, SBP Policy Rate, KIBOR, CPI); - generating structured technical and statistical features; - training multiple prediction models (baselines, tree ensembles, and future architectures); - performing walk-forward backtesting without look-ahead bias; - respecting PSX market microstructure (circuit breakers, lock limits, and transaction costs); - comparing models against simple baselines and market benchmarks (KSE-100); - generating stock forecasts with explainable factors; - recording prediction history for calibration and drift tracking; - presenting predictions, metrics, and explanations through an application dashboard. The first version will only support a small configurable universe of PSX stocks (e.g., KSE-30 / KSE-100 liquid blue chips). Do not attempt to ingest all PSX-listed securities at once. --- # 3. Data Principles & Storage Architecture Maintain distinct storage layers: ### Raw data Raw downloaded information must be preserved in immutable form without transformation wherever practical: ```text data/raw/prices/ data/raw/news/ data/raw/announcements/ data/raw/macro/ ``` ### Processed data Normalized, validated, and deduplicated records: ```text data/processed/prices/ data/processed/corporate_actions/ ``` ### Features Machine-learning-ready datasets: ```text data/features/ ``` ### Predictions Store every generated forecast permanently: ```text data/predictions/ ``` Use **Parquet** for large structured datasets. Use **DuckDB** as the local analytical and query layer over Parquet files. --- # 9. Price Data Schema & PSX Market Microstructure Normalized daily stock records must support: ```text symbol VARCHAR trade_date DATE open DOUBLE high DOUBLE low DOUBLE close DOUBLE adjusted_close DOUBLE volume BIGINT dividend_amount DOUBLE DEFAULT 0.0 split_ratio DOUBLE DEFAULT 1.0 is_upper_lock BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE is_lower_lock BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE source VARCHAR collected_at TIMESTAMP ``` ### Critical PSX Adjustments 1. **Ex-Dividend & Corporate Actions Adjustment:** - PSX is a high-dividend-yield market. Unadjusted close prices create artificial price drops on ex-dividend and ex-bonus dates. - Both raw `close` and `adjusted_close` must be maintained. Features and return calculations must utilize dividend/split-adjusted prices to prevent false breakdown signals. 2. **Circuit Breakers & Upper/Lower Locks:** - PSX implements daily price limits (typically ±7.5% or 1 PKR, whichever is higher). - Flag sessions where a stock hits **Upper Lock** (`high == low == close` at +7.5%) or **Lower Lock** (-7.5%). - In trading simulations and backtests, buy orders cannot execute at the close of an upper lock, and sell orders cannot execute at the close of a lower lock. ### Data Validation Rules: - symbol exists in configured universe; - trade_date is valid; - OHLC values are positive; - high >= open, high >= close, high >= low; - low <= open, low <= close; - volume >= 0; - duplicate symbol/date rows are strictly rejected; - log all rejected invalid rows without silently corrupting data. --- # 10. Critical Time-Series Rule & Look-Ahead Prevention Never perform random train/test splitting for financial time-series data. Financial time-series data must use **strict chronological splitting** and **walk-forward validation**: - Expanding-window evaluation - Rolling-window evaluation Future information must never influence historical model features: - No future prices or forward-peeking moving averages. - No future macroeconomic indicators (use release dates, not observation dates). - No news published after session close/prediction timestamp. - No retrospective data revisions. Prevent look-ahead bias throughout every feature and transformation. --- # 11. News & Market Session Alignment (PSX Calendar) Every news or announcement record must contain: ```text id VARCHAR source VARCHAR headline VARCHAR body VARCHAR published_at TIMESTAMP collected_at TIMESTAMP company_symbols LIST[VARCHAR] event_type VARCHAR sentiment DOUBLE relevance DOUBLE usable_from TIMESTAMP session_target_date DATE ``` ### PSX Trading Hours & Friday Split Schedule: - **Monday – Thursday:** 09:15 PKT to 15:30 PKT (single continuous session). - **Friday Session 1:** 09:15 PKT to 12:00 PKT. - **Friday Prayer Intermission:** 12:00 PKT to 14:30 PKT (Market Closed). - **Friday Session 2:** 14:30 PKT to 16:30 PKT. ### News Alignment Rules (`usable_from`): - News published during market hours is aligned to the earliest subsequent trading session or the current session if received before cut-off. - News published during Friday prayer intermission (12:00–14:30 PKT) becomes `usable_from` Friday Session 2. - News published after market close (15:30 Mon-Thu, 16:30 Fri) or over the weekend is aligned to the **next trading day's open**. --- # 12. Prediction Targets Do not attempt to predict an exact stock price directly. Initial prediction targets: ### Target A — Next-Session Direction (Binary or 3-Class) - **Binary Direction:** ```text 1 = next adjusted close > current adjusted close 0 = next adjusted close <= current adjusted close ``` - **Thresholded 3-Class Target (Noise & Friction Filter):** To filter out micro-noise within the bid-ask spread and transaction costs (~0.25%): ```text UP = return > +0.75% NEUTRAL = -0.75% <= return <= +0.75% DOWN = return < -0.75% ``` ### Target B — Five-Session Future Return ```text (adjusted_close[t+5] / adjusted_close[t]) - 1 ``` All targets must be generated with strict exclusion of future observations from the input feature vector. --- # 13. First Models & Modeling Progression Implement models in the following sequence: ### Step 1: Baselines 1. Majority-class classifier 2. Previous-day-direction naive baseline 3. Simple moving-average crossover strategy 4. Regularized Logistic Regression (Ridge/Lasso) ### Step 2: Tabular ML Models 5. Random Forest Classifier/Regressor 6. XGBoost Classifier/Regressor 7. LightGBM (where appropriate) ### Step 3: Deep Learning (Only after Phase 10 is complete and stable) 8. LSTM / GRU recurrent models 9. Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN) 10. Temporal Transformers Do not implement deep learning in early phases. --- # 14. Model Evaluation & Backtesting Metrics ### Classification Metrics: - Accuracy, Balanced Accuracy - Precision, Recall, F1-Score (macro & weighted) - ROC-AUC & Precision-Recall AUC - Brier Score (probability calibration) - Confusion Matrix ### Regression Metrics: - Mean Absolute Error (MAE) - Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) - Mean Directional Accuracy (MDA) - $R^2$ Score ### Realistic PSX Trading Simulation Metrics: - Total Return & Annualized Return (CAGR) - Sharpe Ratio & Sortino Ratio - Maximum Drawdown (MDD) & Drawdown Duration - Win Rate & Profit Factor - Turnover & Trade Count - **Slippage & Transaction Costs:** Deduct estimated PSX friction (~0.15% broker commission + SECP/CDC/NCCPL charges + Capital Gains Tax). - **Execution Filter:** Reject hypothetical fills on stocks locked at upper limit (for buy) or lower limit (for sell). Compare models against: - Buy and Hold - KSE-100 Benchmark Index - Simple SMA baseline - Naive direction baseline --- # 15. Feature Engineering Implement modular feature generators: 1. **Price & Return Features:** - 1-day, 3-day, 5-day, 10-day, 20-day log and percentage returns. 2. **Moving Averages & Oscillators:** - SMA / EMA (5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200) - RSI (14-period) - MACD (12, 26, 9) & MACD histogram - Rate of Change (ROC) 3. **Volatility Features:** - Rolling standard deviation (10, 20, 60 days) - Average True Range (ATR) - Bollinger Bands & Bollinger Band Width 4. **Volume Features:** - Volume SMA & relative volume (Volume / 20-day Volume SMA) - On-Balance Volume (OBV) - Volume change percentage 5. **PSX Relative Market Features:** - Stock return vs. KSE-100 return - Rolling beta vs. KSE-100 (60-day window) - Sector relative strength All feature computations must be deterministic, unit-tested, and strictly free of look-ahead bias. --- # 16. News Features Do not feed unstructured text directly into the price prediction model. Construct structured news signals: - Sentiment score (-1.0 to +1.0) - News velocity (count of articles in last 24h, 72h) - Positive / negative headline count - Official corporate announcement indicator - Categorized event flags (earnings, dividend, bonus, regulatory, discovery, interest rate) - NLP processing must remain a standalone, decoupled subsystem. --- # 17.
